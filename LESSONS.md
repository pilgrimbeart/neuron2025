# Lessons

What we tried, what worked, what didn't and why, so settled questions aren't reopened. Principles first, then an index of capabilities, then the details: the capability experiments (the current approach), then the earlier work evolving the robot's cell rule. The hand-designed system's own lessons are in `old_manual_gates/LESSONS.md`.

## Principles

- **Local behaviour only, no global god.** No global clock, no broadcast, no harness switching the cells' behaviour. The only things from outside are the body's inputs (sensor pulses, taste).
- **No hidden scale.** A cell knows only its own state and its neighbours'; never the sheet's size or its position in it.
- **Any size.** Every rule must work on a sheet of any size; only the time taken may grow with it. A fixed wait, a cap on a count, a range or threshold tuned to one sheet is a hidden size, and so is any loss per step, however small (it compounds over the length). Test every capability at two very different sizes (e.g. 49 and 512).
- **Stochastic updates only.** Every cell updates at random times, never in lockstep. Lockstep caused artefacts twice: an evolved sheet flipped every cell every tick and steered by the phase, and in `paths.py` poisoning set the distance map flickering in a checkerboard. (The lizard NCA updates each cell with probability 0.5 for the same reason.) The robot's `cell.py` still updates in lockstep and must change before more robot work.
- **Robust to noise.** Every rule must survive some noise in its cells' numbers.
- **Inputs spike.** Sparse kicks, never continuous levels.
- **Random inputs, nothing crafted.** Learning must work with events in random order, not a training sequence designed to make it work.
- **Symmetry must be broken, and noise breaks it.** Identical cells in a regular sheet can't choose between equal options (a route, a corner, a handedness). Something local and random (a tag from birth, starting noise) breaks the tie, and the rule then has to make the choice stick.
- **Excitable media percolate.** In a connected threshold medium a pulse either dies or floods everything; tuning the threshold only moves the boundary. A flood fires every actuator at once, so their pushes cancel.
- **Reward needs variability.** Deterministic trials give the same outcome every time, so reward has nothing to select between.
- **No god in the creature's learning; any help in finding the creature.** The creature learns within its life by local cells and its body's inputs only. Finding such a creature may use anything: hand design, curricula, stages, seeding. Whether it could have evolved unaided is a later question.
- **Still to check:** a cell's chance of updating is per tick, not per unit of time (at tick size 0.5 cells update twice as often per unit of time), so results agree in ticks rather than in time.

# Part 1: capability experiments

"Can a uniform sheet do X?", one at a time: for each, find the simplest mechanism, test it against noise, random update times, tick size and size, and record the pros, cons and dead ends. Once enough capabilities work, compose them: the organism is designed as a sequence of developmental stages (lay down a coordinate system, grow paths between the places it defines, then learn on them), every cell running the same rule throughout and switching stage by local "this stage's work is done here" triggers, as embryos do with one genome; never durations, since a fixed wait hides an assumption about size.

## A new-born creature (`creature.py`)

- **The story, all local, no timers between stages.** A 49×49 brain: the frame names the organs (R at the north edge's middle, B its neighbour along the edge, A at the south edge's middle, V at north-east) by about tick 250; the body mounts its sensors and thrusters on those cells (the head is wherever the sheet put north); two path channels wire R and B to A and V, and the first pulses reach a thruster by about 360; then it moves and learns. The body: a square; R and B at the head see their colour straight ahead; A at the tail pushes forward; V, at the front corner, pushes back off-centre, so it backs and turns; the V cell also fires now and then by itself (a tumble). Taste arrives at R and B. 6,000 ticks take about 15 seconds headless; live at 60 ticks a second the whole story takes about two minutes.
- **Result (49×49, 4 seeds × both poison colours, 8,000 ticks).** Poison's share of meals in the thirds of life after moving began: 44%, 19%, 17%; final balance poison 0.08, food 1.00. Without learning: 50%, 44%, 49%. At tick size 0.5: 38%, 19%, 26%.
- **What the body needed (with learning off, scored by meals; each traced to a cause).**
  - **A narrow view.** With the sensors' wide cosine field they were nearly always active (a median strength of 0.71), so A pushed whatever lay ahead and the creature sailed past blocks. Cosine to the power 20 (about ±15°) makes it run only at what is straight ahead, and tumble otherwise: run-and-tumble, as E. coli.
  - **A wide mouth.** It can only drive straight, and a block slightly off its line slides out of view as it nears; with a 1.5-unit mouth at the head almost every run missed. The whole head end (4 units) eats.
  - **A tempo to suit a slow brain.** A pulse takes about 100 ticks to cross the 49 brain, so the creature acts on what it saw 100 ticks ago. Frequent tumbles, large turns (40° per V) and stray V pulses (one in four at a born balance of 0.75) broke its runs before they arrived. Gentle turns, rare tumbles, strong pushes, a born balance of 0.9, and sensing fast enough that the input cell sends at its limit: about 32 meals in 6,000 ticks without learning, from 0–2.
- **Taste at once.** With the taste arriving 10 ticks after the meal, the food sensor often kicked in between, its trace ended higher, and food took the blame (food's balance fell to 0.81, food meals halved). With 2 ticks: as above.
- **Telling food from poison: the learner was at the body's ceiling (24 lives per variant).** Of the poison eaten after learning, 23 of 25 meals came with both colours in view just before: the food drives A, the poison V, and the pushes add. But with fixed perfect balances (poison 0, food 1, no learning) the body still ate as much poison (31–33% of meals) as the learner (28%): the learner was at the ceiling, and the rest is the body. What raised the ceiling, with no new numbers in any cell:
  - **Corridor sensors:** the sensors see what lies in the corridor the mouth will sweep (ahead, within its width), not a cone: the narrow cone missed blocks a little to the side, close up, that the wide mouth then ate unseen. Perfect balances 33% → 20–23%.
  - **A gentler body (strength 0.7):** after a sighting about 6 pushes are already in flight (pulses take about 100 ticks to cross the brain), so a strong body lunges on old news. Perfect balances 19% → 10%, with most of the food (136 meals against 170); at 0.5 no better and half the food.
  - **Didn't help (within noise or worse):** braking on V, backing off harder, turning more, ignoring A for a while after V.
  - **Result with learning:** poison 14% of meals over the last two thirds of life, 7% in the last third (no learning: 53%); before, 28%.
  - **A harness trap, again:** a pool's worker processes keep module settings between jobs, so variants run one after another inherited each other's settings (and a "perfect balances" patch for the wrong colour); a fresh process per job (maxtasksperchild=1) fixed it.
- **18 blocks of each colour (from 12):** about twice the food (226 meals over the last two thirds of 24 lives, from 114), poison 17% of meals (14% with 12; a denser arena puts more poison in the way; 49% without learning). Now the default.
- **A disgust reflex (DISGUST):** a bad taste lingers in the mouth for 40 ticks, kicking R and B (at the sensors' rate) straight into the avoid route, so the creature recoils after eating poison: a visible reaction, and it helps (poison 12% of meals in the last third, against 23% without; 24 lives). No new numbers in any cell. The live view shows "yuck!" and the poisoned colour's balance highlighted as it falls.
- **B places itself, no god.** At first the body mounted B on R's east neighbour (a lookup outside the cells). Now B is a named place like any other: "the north middle, one cell east". A place may carry an offset in cells, and a cell is 1 / width across, which every cell knows from its own counts (from the west plus from the east), so the nearest cell to that place is exactly R's east neighbour at any size: no new shared number. Doing this also showed the harness had been snapping each place to the nearest grid point before the cells saw it (using the sheet's size); now the cells get the raw fractions, and on an even sheet, where the north middle falls between two cells, ties go to the cell to the west (or south), so exactly one cell takes each name (right at 24, 25, 48 and 49).
- **At 97×97 with corridor sensors and strength 0.7 (24 lives, 16,000 ticks):** poison 23% of meals over the last two thirds (49: 14%), with plenty of food (227 meals); at 0.35, 20% and half the food. So the same body serves both sizes, a little less sharply on the bigger, slower brain.
- **At 97×97: the learning is right, the behaviour needs a gentler body.** With the same body the balances came out near perfect (poison 0.01–0.03, food about 0.97) but poison stayed about half of what it ate, in every seed. Not pulse delivery (pulses reach V at 97, routes in proportion: 96 and 28 steps against 48 and 14). The cause: an input cell sends at most one pulse every 18 brain ticks, so the pulses in flight on a route grow with its length; after a food sighting a 97 brain still has about 12 pushes on their way (49: 6), and the creature overshoots into whatever lies beyond. Slowing the world (sensing, tumbles, drag) didn't help; making each push and turn half as strong (TEMPO 0.5) did: poison 50%, 22%, 33%. A bigger brain needs a gentler body; the body here is tuned to its brain by hand (TEMPO), not by the cells.

## Learning which colour is poison (`learn.py`)

- **The set-up: one of each thing.** A red and a blue sensor's input cells by one edge, an APPROACH and an AVOID output by the other, wired by two breadcrumb channels (one per output, both inputs sources in each: four routes, crossing freely). The body and world: a block appears, red or blue at random; while in view its sensor kicks its input cell; the first output to receive 3 pulses wins; approach means eaten and, 10 later, tasted at both input cells (bad if poison). Next block a random while after the pulses in flight have arrived; nothing waits a time that depends on size.
- **The rule, in the input cell (where the paths start and taste arrives).** Two numbers: a balance b (each pulse goes towards APPROACH with chance b, else AVOID) and a trace (1 when its sensor last kicked it, fading over 50). A taste moves b by 0.6 × taste × trace, scaled to stay between 0 and 1. Three factors: pre (the trace), post (a meal follows only approach, so the taste arriving is itself the sign approach won), and the taste. One number for "punish towards and reward away": lowering b does both. The learning lives at the input because routes from the two inputs merge further on.
- **Result (49×49, 8 seeds × both poison colours, tick sizes 1 and 0.5, 30 blocks).** In the second half of lives poison was approached 7–8% of the time and food 99–100%; poison eaten 2.4 times per life (at most 4); final balance poison about 0.15, food 1.00. Without learning (rate 0): both colours about 55%, poison eaten 7.4–7.8 times. Learning takes one to three bad tastes.
- **Crosstalk, the price of a time trace:** in 6 of 16 lives a poison taste also lowered the food colour's balance for a while (food had been seen just before), always recovered by later good tastes. Sending taste back along the path that drove the meal (like the acknowledgements) would credit only the inputs that actually drove it; untried.
- **`paths.py` change:** an end kick no longer has to send a pulse (KICK_SENDS); the world can make a source send (a sensor). Paths now carry only signal, so an unused route is forgotten and re-grown on request (sinks and sources ask as before), rather than held by maintenance pulses.
- **At 512×512 (choice world, 2 seeds × 20 blocks).** With the current settings, the same as at 49: poison approached 2 of 11 times in the second halves (16% at 49), food 9 of 9; final balances poison 0.40 and 0.15, food 0.96 and 0.93. With the first settings (inputs apart, no comparison) 512 had shown poison approached 3 of 9 times despite balances of 0.07–0.08; the suspected cause (a race favouring the shorter route) was wrong: measured, both routes are equally long (410 and 409 steps; a diagonal step counts as one), deliver 96–98% of pulses at 512 as at 49, and take about 2.2 ticks per cell. That earlier result no longer appears, and with samples this small it may have been chance; not traced further.
- **The line world: continuous behaviour, both colours in view.** The robot on a line, blocks ahead; each pulse at APPROACH a step forward, at AVOID a step back; sensors kick at a rate rising as their nearest block nears. Three things were needed, each traced to a cause:
  1. **An innate pull (born balance 0.75).** At 0.5 forward and back cancel, the robot random-walks, blocks drift out of range, food is hardly ever tasted, and nothing is reinforced.
  2. **Credit by comparison (CONTRAST).** Going forward approaches every block, so when poison is eaten, food is usually just behind it and its trace is up too: at bad tastes the eaten colour's trace averaged about 0.7 and the other's about 0.5, and food's balance fell with poison's (to about 0.4; a shorter trace didn't help). Moving each balance by how far its trace exceeds its neighbour's (the two inputs side by side, a sensor organ) gives the blame to the one more recently active: lateral inhibition in credit. Local only because the inputs are neighbours.
  3. **A flavour kick (FLAVOUR):** a block being eaten kicks its colour's sensor once (the food in the mouth, sensed strongly), so the eaten colour's trace is reliably the higher. A body property.
  With all three (trace 10, rate 1, 4 seeds × both poison colours): second half of lives, poison eaten 24%, food 68%; final balance poison 0.33, food 0.77. The ceiling, fixed ideal balances (0.05 and 0.95) with no learning: poison 7%, food 72% (in one dimension, poison in front blocks the food). So food is near its ceiling; poison is avoided only partly, as its balance settles near 0.3 once it is rarely tasted.
  In the choice world the same settings give no crosstalk at all (food's balance never fell in 16 lives, against 6 of 16 before), but learn more slowly: 5.3 poison meals per life (2.4 before), poison approached 16% in the second half (8% before), from the innate pull and the shorter trace.
- **The input cell's rule is in `learn.py`, outside the path kernel** (one cell, two numbers); moving it into the shared cell rule comes with putting it all in the robot.

## Development in stages (`develop.py`)

- **The first composed organism: frame, then points, then paths, all local.** Each cell runs the frame rule and three copies of the path rule, one per side of an equilateral triangle about the centre (radius 0.3 of the body). A cell nearest one of the triangle's corners, with a consistent frame, names itself point 1, 2 or 3; in channel k point k is a source and the next point a sink; named points pulse at random. 49×49: points by about tick 190, all sides joined by 215–270, silent at the end (3 seeds). 129×129: points by about 510, sides by 560–660, silent.
- **No timers between stages.** No cell is a source or sink until it has a name, and none has a name until its frame is consistent. The hand-over came for free from the guards each capability already had.
- **A channel per target.** Each side heads for a particular point, so each needs its own channel (one shared channel would join each point only to its nearest): the crossing lesson again.

## A body frame (`frame.py`)

The task: every cell comes to hold x and y from 0 to 1, and cells name themselves as particular places by them (the middle of the north edge; halfway from the centre to the north-east corner): a head-tail axis, then left-right.

- **What can't be done deterministically.** A square has 8 symmetries. A direction-blind rule from a symmetric start stays symmetric: it can find the centre, the corners and the edge middles as sets, but not "the north middle" (a choice of 4 rotations) or "north-east" (then 2 reflections). That takes 3 bits made by chance (spontaneous symmetry breaking; in embryos often an outside cue) and broadcast, at least one crossing of the sheet.
- **Minimum numbers per cell.** Two numbers give a unique position, but the references matter: distances to two edge middles give the centre and the north-east corner the same pair (a mirror in the line through the references); counts from two adjacent corners don't. Naming a place given as a fraction of the body, without knowing its size, needs coordinates that are themselves fractions.
- **The rule, in sequence, each step simple.**
  1. Corners know they are corners (three neighbours).
  2. Election: every cell holds the smallest corner tag heard (a random name from birth); the corner whose own tag survives is the origin (0, 0). No ties; one crossing.
  3. The origin chooses the axes: its edge neighbour with the smaller tag starts a sign +1 along its edge, the other −1; the corner +1 reaches is (1, 0) and relays +2; the corner +2 reaches is (1, 1); the corner −1 reaches is (0, 1) and relays −2. Every edge is labelled with no second race. (A first version relaying "+" and "−" and calling the corner that got both (1, 1) raced: the far corner could hear "+" first and send it back along the "−" edge.)
  4. Whole-number counts in from each edge; x = count from west / (west + east), y likewise.
  5. A cell names itself a place if its (x, y) is nearer the place's than any neighbour's.
- **Five shared numbers, from ten.** What a cell's neighbours read of it: the smallest corner tag it has heard, and its four counts. The key that work was made under is just the smallest tag heard before this update; the edge signs and corner labels became zeros in the counts (a cell on the south edge holds 0 as its count from the south; zeros run along the edges, and a corner turns the incoming zero into its next edge's; whole numbers keep the zeros exact under noise); x and y are kept to itself, and a neighbour's are worked out from its counts. Same results as before: right by 200–250 on 49×49, 100–150 on 25, 400 on 97, 2,000 on 512; jitter 0.1, updates a quarter of ticks and tick size 0.5 fine, 0.2 fails as before; origins 11/10/10/9 and handedness 24/16 in 40 seeds. One trap on the way: an edge cell next to a corner took both of the corner's zeros (its own edge's and the next), so the next edge's zero ran down the wrong edge; it takes a corner's zeros only if it has none yet. The averaging rule (a recorded dead end) went with the corner labels. Fewer is hard: one number must break the symmetry, and fractions need two counts per axis; averaging could give x and y in two, but needs the edges labelled, is warped near held points and slow (time grows with the size squared).
- **A fixed wait is a hidden size; keying removes it.** The origin first waited 300 ticks so that corners wrongly believing they had won (each does, until a smaller tag arrives) wouldn't act: enough on 49×49, but on 512×512 the election takes about 1,000 ticks to cross, all four corners acted as origins, and the frame was mislabelled for good. Now nothing waits: every corner acts at once on its belief, and everything a cell builds on the election (sign, counts) is keyed by the winner it believes in; it uses a neighbour's sign or count only under the same key, and drops its own when a smaller tag arrives. A false origin's work is erased as the truth spreads. One more number per cell.
- **Knowing when your coordinates are right: a local check.** Naming only once all four counts had arrived still let about 3,200 cells claim a place on 512×512 while the edges were being labelled (counts from the labelled parts only: finite but wrong), enough to freeze the live view. In a finished frame west + east is the same for every cell (the width), and likewise south + north; so a cell names itself only when its two sums equal its neighbours'. No size needed, and no false claims.
- **Results.** Exact coordinates everywhere, both places named by exactly their own cell, and staying right: by tick 200 on 49×49 (550 with the old wait), 2,100 on 512×512; 4 seeds, tick sizes 1 and 0.5, jitter 0.1, cells updating a quarter of ticks, sizes 25 and 97. Jitter 0.2 fails (noise beyond half a step defeats whole-number counts). Fair: over 40 seeds each corner became the origin 9–11 times; handedness 24 and 16.
- **Averaging was simpler to state but failed.** Corners held, every other cell averaging its neighbours' x and y: four held points have little grip on a 2D sheet (pinning a rubber sheet at four points tents it only near the pins), so most of it sat near 0.5 (x across the middle only 0.41–0.59). Edge cells averaging only along their edge (a 1D wire gives a straight ramp) would fix it, at the cost of a special case. It was also slow (error 0.47 after 6,000 ticks, 0.01 after 60,000 on 49×49; settling time grows faster than the square of the size; over-relaxation helped little and diverged beyond 1.8) and too warped to name places (near the north middle y changed about 0.0005 per cell, so 5–7 cells found themselves locally nearest). Waves cross once, in time proportional to the size, and give unwarped fractions.

## Coordinates from two shared numbers (`coords.py`)

From an external research brief ("diffuse, remember, then anchor"), reproduced here. The question: can a sheet form a coordinate map if each cell shares only its two coordinates?

- **The constraints.** Each cell shares only u and v, and sees only their averages over its side neighbours (4 inside, 3 on an edge, 2 at a corner). It knows its own number of neighbours and may keep private numbers; it knows no positions, directions, neighbours' identities or kinds, size, clock or global statistics. One random cell is activated at a time. u and v start as random noise.
- **The rule, in a sentence:** every cell averages; a corner also watches its own changes fade, saves its (u, v) once the fade is a clean exponential, and, after the rest has faded much further, holds the saved pair for good. In detail (step H = 0.001; a private age grows by H per activation):
  1. Every cell moves (u, v) by H × (neighbours' sum − own × count): diffusion.
  2. A corner fits the log of its proposed change's size against its age (a leaky regression weighted towards recent ages, weight min(1, 8H / age)): the slope is minus the fade rate r, the fit quality R². Its confidence grows while age × r > 10 and R² > 0.98.
  3. At confidence 0.9 it saves its current pair, its age and r, once, and carries on diffusing.
  4. When (age − age at saving) × r > 8, it restores the saved pair and holds it for good.
- **Why it works.** From noise, diffusion wipes out short-scale structure first; what survives longest is the two slowest patterns a square holds (west against east and south against north, which fade at the same rate, about π² / n²), mixed at random, and these already form an unfolded map. A corner can tell when only they are left, without knowing the size: the fade becomes a clean exponential at that rate. Saving then holding lets the corners keep that pattern while everything else fades by another e^8; with the four corners held, every other cell averaging settles to the harmonic map between them, which is unfolded whenever the four saved points form a convex quadrilateral in the corners' order round the sheet. Orientation, handedness and scale come from the noise: no compass, no election, no edge labels. Saving and publishing are separated because holding at once disturbed corners still measuring and sometimes collapsed a dimension (from the brief).
- **Reproduced (baseline, no ongoing noise).** Right (four corners saved and held; every triangle of the map turning the same way; the edge not crossing itself; the map not flat) at every size from 2 to 32, 4 seeds each (44 of 44), judged on the map the held corners imply (solved directly, outside the cells); and the live map, left running as long again after the corners held, right too (9 of 9 at 8, 16 and 24). Activations per cell grow with the size squared (about 2.2 million at 32×32: 33 seconds compiled).
- **At 49×49:** right (one seed; the live map still right after running half as long again), after 5.2 million activations per cell (20 minutes compiled).
- **Ongoing noise breaks it at a ten-thousandth of its own spread.** Adding noise to every update (16×16, 3 seeds): 1e-11, all right; 1e-10, corners still held but the live map folded (0 of 2), since the map's spread is only about 1e-7 and millions of updates add the noise up; 3e-10, no corner ever trusts the fade (it is buried in the noise from the start). For comparison, `frame.py` tolerates noise of a tenth of a whole count step.
- **Holding only the corners turns a near-perfect grid into a star.** Watched live (`python coords.py --watch`): just before the corners hold, the map formed by the slowest patterns is an almost even, slightly rotated grid; after they hold, the harmonic map between four points bunches most of the sheet in the middle with long spikes out to the corners (unfolded, but far more warped than the grid it came from). The same warping as averaging with the corners held, earlier.
- **Making it robust, still with two shared numbers and no knowledge of a cell's own geometry (16×16, 8 seeds per variant; switches in `coords.py`).**
  - **Why it is fragile.** Averaging purifies the slowest pattern and shrinks it at the same rate (on a square the next-slowest fades exactly twice as fast), so purity e^K costs a shrinkage e^K; and the corners watch their change (own value against the neighbours' average), which magnifies fine-grained noise and shrinks the smooth pattern. The brief waits K ≈ 18 in all; the noise it can stand is roughly e^−K over the square root of the updates per cell: about 1e-11, as measured.
  - **Saving sooner (fade e^5 to trust, e^3 to wait):** 8 of 8 right, half the time, map 800 times bigger. Sooner than about e^5 fails even without noise: the rate estimates are still poor (44–126% apart).
  - **Boosting:** a cell's change divided by the fade rate is its offset from the sheet's average (for the slowest patterns), and multiplying by e^(rate × age) undoes the fade, so a cell can save its coordinate at the scale the pattern had at the start, without knowing the average. Corners only, boosted: 8 of 8, map spread 0.06 (from 1e-7); noise tolerated while forming rises from 1e-10 to 1e-8 per update (1e-7 fails: the corners never trust the fade).
  - **Everyone remembers (every cell watches its own fade and saves its boosted coordinate):** the published map is the slowest-pattern grid, not a star, but each cell's e^(rate × age) multiplies its rate error by about 10 (rates 2.3% apart), and neighbours fold. A longer-memory fit (LEAK 2) gets rates within 0.22%, but cells take longer to trust it, so cells still measuring are disturbed by neighbours publishing: wait longer before publishing (e^6). Still 12–25% of triangles folded, scattered: each cell's own estimate is too noisy against the spacing between neighbours, worst near the edges where the slowest pattern is flattest.
  - **Soft holding fixes that:** once published, a cell moves to 2% of its saved pair plus 98% of its neighbours' average (a sheet pinned everywhere, softly): 7 of 8 right, warp 0.126 (a near-even grid; the corner-held star is 0.31), map spread 0.08. Once formed it tolerates noise of 1e-4 per update (7 of 8; 1e-3 fails), since every cell keeps being pulled back to its memory.
  - **Didn't help:** bigger steps (H 0.05, 0.2), fitting the fade on a running average of the change, trusting the fade on a looser fit; braking the corner-held map with noise only after forming (the star bunches the middle so tightly that noise of 1e-4 folds it).
  - **Verdict so far:** keeping a map can be made robust; forming one this way can't yet. Every variant needs noise below about 1e-8 of the starting spread while the map forms, because the information is carried by a pattern that must shrink by e^5 or more and is measured through a difference that magnifies noise.
- **Growing the pattern instead of catching it as it fades: robust to noise of 1e-2 (prototype; growth rate still set by the observer).** Experiments in numpy, side neighbours' averages only, noise in every update (prototypes in `experiments/coords/growth/`):
  - **Corner-only amplification fails whatever its form.** Corners pushing away from their neighbours' average, clamped into a square, lock onto local noise (13 of 16 runs: every assignment of corners is self-consistent, so the first one wins); onto a circle, all corners end at one angle opposite everything else; bistable growth everywhere almost always ends uniform. A linear feedback from a cell's own history cannot work either: for any such feedback the sheet's average, which must stay neutral, grows at least as fast as the large pattern.
  - **Two copies at two speeds.** Each cell starts u = v (its own random number); u averages fast, v slowly. Both keep the sheet's average, so v − u has none: it measures the large pattern as a value, not as a weak difference. Caught as it fades, it tolerates noise of about 1e-5 (n² better than through the change), still fading-limited.
  - **The breakthrough: grow it.** v also grows its difference from u (rate K, saturating at a fixed size), and u follows v (rate 1.2 K, which keeps the sheet's average of v − u from running away). With K between about 1.24 and 2.24 times v's slowest fade rate, only the slowest pair of patterns grows, from the noise itself (noise keeps reseeding them): at 16×16 with noise of 1e-3 and even 1e-2 per update, the pattern grows to size 0.4–0.6 and is over 99% one straight west-east or south-north pattern.
  - **The second coordinate:** a second pass, with its saturation also limited by the first pattern (size² of both under a ceiling), always picks the other direction (4 of 4).
  - **Exact coordinates from the patterns:** corners hold x and y at 0 or 1 by the signs of the two patterns (at a corner both are at their extremes, so the signs are robust); every other cell averages, except that an edge cell holds whichever coordinate its neighbours' average puts clearly nearest 0 or 1 (within 0.45, and nearer than the other by 0.05), at that value. A star grows from the corners and straightens edge by edge into exact linear coordinates. Two traps on the way: edge cells deciding at once froze into a patchwork (wait until the neighbours' average is off 0.5), and cells next to a corner, equally near both of its values, cascaded a wrong choice along the edge (require a clear margin; the middles of the edges decide first). Result: 16 of 16 right at 16×16 and 24×24 with noise of 1e-3 (largest coordinate error 0.02), 12–13 of 16 at 1e-2 (the failures were the second pattern not fully grown in the fixed time allowed).
  - **Still open: the cells must find the growth rate themselves.** It must sit within a factor of about 1.8 of the slowest fade rate, which depends on the size. Each cell can measure that fade from v − u, but only from changes (with cells updating one at a time, u and v drift to slightly different averages, so v − u settles to a constant, not 0): from v − u at doubling ages, three remembered values give the rate. Without noise 99% of cells get it (median 1.01 of the truth); with noise, too few cells, and some badly wrong, because the slowest pattern in white starting noise is only a 1/n share of it.
- **Cells finding the growth rate themselves (coarse to fine; prototypes in `experiments/coords/growth/`).** Each cell may use its own clock (its age, in its own updates), never a shared one.
  - **Measuring the fade of the starting noise fails under noise:** white noise puts only a 1/n share into the slowest pattern, which reaches the noise floor within a couple of fade times; and v − u settles to a constant offset, not to 0 (with cells updating one at a time, u and v drift to slightly different averages), so the fade must be read from changes (v − u at doubling ages, three values giving the rate), and early, cleanly fading transients mislead a fit (estimates hundreds of times too high).
  - **Per-cell control fails:** cells stepping their rate down until their pattern fades disagree wildly (0.01 to 80 times the right rate), because a cell whose rate is too low is kept going by its neighbours; stopping when the pattern is steady stops too early (a saturated pattern is steady too).
  - **A shared level as the channel fails:** adding the same amount to u and v leaves v − u alone only if it is the same everywhere; votes differ from cell to cell, and u and v average at different speeds, so votes leak into v − u.
  - **Coarse to fine, one round, works more often than not:** (A) grow at a fixed high rate, which turns noise into O(1) domains (a single straight wall 6 of 16 times, otherwise a quarter-circle domain at a corner, which is also stable); (B) switch growth off and let them fade: measured on O(1) domains, the cells' fade estimates land on the truth (per-run medians 0.95–1.07 at 16, 24, 32, noise 1e-2), though only 25–50% of cells get one in time and about a third of those are off; (C) grow the first pattern at 1.6 times the cell's own estimate (no estimate, no growth: neighbours carry it), then the second from fresh random numbers, damped by the first, then the coordinate pass. Every cell on its own: 24×24, 15 of 16 right at noise 1e-3 and 14 of 16 at 1e-2; 32×32, 14 and 10 of 16; 16×16 poor (the second pattern takes the first's direction, or the first comes out tilted). Results swing with small changes (accepting estimates only after a fade of 4 of their own time units, though it removes most bad estimates, gave 11 and 4 of 16 at 24): the weak point is that the growth rate differs from cell to cell.
- **Corner flags: the first robust two-number solution (prototype; `experiments/coords/flags/`).** Averages only, two shared numbers, no geometry, noise of 1e-2 in every update. The rule:
  - **Inside cells** always set u and v to their neighbours' averages.
  - **Corners** start with u at 0 and, at a random moment when nothing is happening, raise a flag (u = 10, for good). Each flag spreads by averaging, and every other corner sees its neighbours' average rise and level off: by the flag's share at that corner, smallest from the diagonal corner (a few percent of the flag; diagonal over adjacent 0.67 at 8×8, 0.75 at 16, 0.85 at 97, the two adjacent always equal). A corner records the order of events and the three rises: no one before me, (0,0); first rise the smallest, (1,1); else (0,1) if my diagonal raised before me, (1,0) if not. It keeps its flag up three times the longest rise it watched settle (so the far corners record it), then holds u, v at its label.
  - **Edge cells** hold whichever coordinate their neighbours' average puts clearly nearest 0 or 1 (within 0.45, nearer than the other by 0.05), at that value, once the flags have reached them and gone and their neighbours' u has stopped falling (within 0.03 of a slow private average).
  - Result at 16×16: corners labelled a proper square 7 of 8 times, and those 7 reached exact coordinates (largest error 0.045–0.062, the noise level); the eighth never finished labelling (probably two flags overlapping). Flags alone at 32×32: 7 of 8 proper squares with a stricter settle test. At 8×8 only 4 of 8 (the rest wrong corner labels: steps settle quickly on a small sheet, so flags overlap more often): collisions are the main open failure.
  - **Traps on the way, each traced:** the noise makes slow wobbles on the same timescale as the rises, so flags must be big (10: a constant of the rule) and the detection threshold relative to the flag; a corner's quiet-level estimate absorbed real rises; "settled" was declared one update after a rise began (the first check must come after the smoothing time); the last corner to raise lowered its flag before the far corners had recorded it; edges gated on v never straightened the edges where y runs 0 to 1; edges gated only on the flags having passed held 1 while u was still falling through it from 10; a latched gate did the same.
  - **Not yet adaptive or robust:** the settle test (fixed 2–5% over a doubling: fails at 48×48; to be replaced by extrapolating each rise's final size from three values at doubling times, Aitken's method), collisions (two flags overlapping: to be recovered from by lowering the flag and retrying with longer waits, as Ethernet does), and the 0.03 stillness tolerance.
  - **Slow:** corners labelled after about 100,000–500,000 ticks at 16×16; estimated 2–5 million at 48×48, against about 200 for the 5-number frame (averaging settles in time growing with the size squared, the four flags go one after another, and the waits between them are long to avoid collisions).
- **Where it stopped (tagged `exp/coords-two-numbers`).** The aim, a design that emergently builds a creature which learns one bit, was met with the 5-number frame; this side project asked whether "where am I" can be done with the theoretical minimum of two shared numbers and averages only, and showed that it can (corner flags), robustly to noise, at a heavy cost in time (about ten thousand times slower than counting waves at 48×48) and in corner logic. Open, if it is ever picked up: collision recovery for flags (lower and retry with longer waits), extrapolated rise sizes (Aitken) instead of the fixed settle test, an adaptive stillness tolerance, then sizes beyond 32. What it taught beyond itself: means-only communication is far weaker than seeing neighbours individually (no minimum, no election, no labels), and a third shared number (an election, with x and y averaged between edges labelled by out-of-range values) would be simple, fast and robust if neighbours could be seen individually.
- **What it costs, against `frame.py`:** 2 shared numbers instead of 5, no election, no edge labels, no knowledge of neighbours' kinds, and only the four corners differ. But the map is warped (held only at the corners), in an arbitrary orientation and scale, and tiny: its spread is about 1e-8 to 1e-6 around the starting mean, so it lives in the last digits of the numbers.
- **Open questions for us:** (1) ongoing noise in the numbers (our rule: robust to noise), when the map is that small; (2) naming places: a cell knows its own (u, v) but not where it sits within the map's range or orientation, which only the corners hold; (3) time: millions of activations per cell (speeding up while all cells know they are in this stage is allowed).

## Knowing where you are (`places.py`)

The task: the four corner cells and the centre (one cell, or four on an even size) identify themselves, all cells starting the same bar noise. The lizard's question in miniature.

- **All positional information comes from the edge.** In a uniform sheet nothing else differs from place to place, so position is read off the boundary (Wolpert's positional information). A cell senses the edge locally: beyond it, it has no neighbour.
- **Counting steps: simplest and most robust to size.** (1) Count steps in from the edge (edge cells 0, others their smallest neighbour's count plus one): the centre has no neighbour higher. (2) The centre holds 0 in a second count, out from it: the corners are the highest. Right in every run (4 seeds, tick sizes 1 and 0.5) within about 300 ticks on 48×48, and stays right; also at 24, 49 and 96 (about 640 ticks), and with cells updating a quarter of ticks.
- **Count out from the centre as a rook moves.** With diagonal steps of √2 the outward count climbs only 0.41 per cell along an edge, too little above the noise, and edge middles claimed to be corners; counting a diagonal step as 2, it climbs a whole step per cell.
- **Whole numbers resist noise.** As real numbers, noise piled up along the counts (the minimum picks out low values): at jitter 0.1 everything failed. Rounded, noise under half a step is wiped out at every step, as digital signals resist noise; and a count must jump to its target (with a chance matching the rate, so the same speed at any tick size) rather than creep, or it sits between whole numbers and rounding becomes a coin toss. Then right at jitter 0.1 in all runs; at 0.2 it fails (the minimum copies a single bad low value onwards).
- **A leaky chemical (one variable): simplest to state, slow.** Every cell makes a little; it spreads by averaging and leaks away beyond the edge; it settles highest at the centre and lowest at the corners. It survives more noise (averaging cancels it) but takes far longer (about 750 ticks on 48×48, 2,750 on 96: diffusion time grows with the square of the size) and flickers more (right 90% of the time on 48×48, 44% with one centre cell, whose dome top is flattest). It needed strong production and corners judged as clear dips (the flat edge middles made false corners otherwise).

## Growing paths (`paths.py`)

### The breadcrumb rule (current)

- **Two tricks make paths simple and robust.** (1) Sinks send waves: an excited cell excites its neighbours, then rests (refractory), so waves only travel outwards and fade at the edges; each wave counts its steps. (2) Each cell remembers which neighbour the wave came from (its breadcrumb, by that neighbour's tag); a source is on the path, and so is any cell a path cell's breadcrumb points to: a path is the breadcrumb trail from a source to the nearest sink. Details: a breadcrumb is kept unless the wave now comes clearly shorter (10%) another way; a cell forgets everything when no wave has come for a while; ends are pulsed and hold their state. Nothing stale can circulate (all information comes fresh from a real sink) and breadcrumbs can't loop (a kept one points to a cell the same wave reached first). Every source joined a sink in every scene (4 seeds, tick sizes 1 and 0.5, jitter); after a sink moved the path re-formed within 150 ticks; with a sink removed, stranded sources re-routed; with all removed, every path vanished. Single paths are straight-line length (44 cells on 48×48, 92 on 96×96); several sources share branches (6 sources, 3 sinks on 96×96: 169 cells). Robust to jitter 20 times the default and to any update rate whose windows still span enough updates (fails at a quarter of ticks).
- **The wave must count its steps; arrival order won't do.** "Remember which neighbour woke you first" joined only about half the time: with random update times, which of two near-equal neighbours wakes first is a coin toss, so breadcrumbs kept switching. A count doesn't depend on when cells happened to update. (Comparing "time left excited" across moments was a trap: it shrinks for everyone, so it must be taken relative to one's own.)
- **How it was found (dead ends).** Waves first gave distances 2–3× too large: a cell that missed a wave later re-excited recovered cells (re-entry, as in heart arrhythmia); fixed by excitation long enough that a cell rarely misses it (16) and refractoriness long enough that a late one finds everyone resting (40). Following the falling distance ("downhill") got stuck in pits of uneven noise; breadcrumbs can't, since they point back along the wave. Choosing the breadcrumb only at the moment of excitation failed (a cell is excited before its own parent 28% of the time); it must watch the whole excited window. Comparing remembered distances made paths meander after a move; comparing ways within the same wave fixed it. A slow fade on path cells bridged frequent breadcrumb switches but left dead chains unravelling; once switches were rare it was dropped. Roughness from birth (once needed to break ties) proved unnecessary: breadcrumbs and their stickiness break ties. Growing from both kinds of end doubled the cells and drew two routes; growing only from sources is enough.

### Paths that persist, quietly

- **Two more tricks (PERSIST).** (3) A sink calls (sends waves) only while hungry, when no pulse has reached it for a while; pulses run along paths (each path cell passes a pulse to the cell its breadcrumb names), so once a path delivers, the sink stops calling and the sheet goes quiet. (4) A path lives as long as its sink thanks it: a sink receiving a pulse sends an acknowledgement back up the path, refreshing the breadcrumbs (like the growth factor a target sends back along the axon: the neurotrophic hypothesis). A path to a vanished sink is no longer thanked and is forgotten. Results (4 seeds, tick sizes 1 and 0.5, noise): once wired, wave activity falls to zero (0.28 of cells excited at any moment without persistence); a removed sink's paths clear; damage breaks a path, its sink calls, the path re-routes, the sheet goes quiet again; a moved sink is re-wired; every scene fully joined 600 ticks after a change. The price is reaction time: a stranded source takes 300–600 ticks to be re-wired (150 without persistence), forgetting a removed sink about 350.
- **What it took.** Refreshing by pulses would keep a path to a dead sink alive, hence acknowledgements. Pulses died where they bunched (a pulse reaching a cell still resting from the last dies): a short rest after passing (2) is enough, since breadcrumbs already stop pulses going backwards. A memory of 100 broke paths on a rare long gap in thanks; 200 didn't. A fed sink stops calling, so new or stranded sources could never be wired: occasional waves from fed sinks spoiled the quiet, so instead a source not thanked for a while sends a bare request wave, and a sink it reaches calls for a while, fed or not. Then a sink no source wanted, alone in calling, re-pointed everyone's breadcrumbs and hijacked working paths; so a cell thanked recently keeps its breadcrumb whatever waves pass (if it works, don't fix it). Loneliness and hunger thresholds of 200 (not 100) stopped occasional false alarms.
- **At any size (tested at 48, 192 and 384).** Three hidden sizes. (1) "No count known" was 1,000, capping path length: now effectively infinite. (2) Sources started not lonely, but on a big sheet the first thanks takes a full round trip: now a source is lonely from birth and asks until first thanked, and 200 then measures gaps between thanks, which don't grow with size. (3) Pulses and acknowledgements were passed on for 8 ticks, and with random update times the next cell missed that window 0.4% of the time: negligible over 44 cells, about half lost over 190, so sources were rarely thanked and sinks kept calling. Passing on for 16 makes a miss about 1 in 65,000. (Bunching still limits how many arrive, but some always do.) One pair across 192×192 joined by tick 600, across 384×384 by 1,200 (paths of 188 and 382 cells); three sources and two sinks by 600 and 2,350; silent once wired; removing the sinks clears every path; 48×48 unchanged.

- **Five shared numbers per channel, from seven.** What neighbours read: the count while in a wave (else none), the breadcrumb while on the path (else none), and whether passing a pulse, a request, or thanks. These two merges hide nothing (neighbours never used a count without the wave, or a breadcrumb without the path), and every result is unchanged (48: all scenes; silent once wired at 192 and at 384 in 4 of 4 lives). Merging the signals themselves failed, each traced: a request shown only when the cell isn't in a wave let request fronts break where waves crossed, and broken fronts in an excitable medium curl into spiral waves that never stop (192: sinks calling 99% of the time while thanked every 22–41 ticks); thanks shown ahead of waves broke wave fronts on the path the same way (noisy for good in 1 of 4 lives at 384, original 0 of 4); thanks shown behind waves went missing during the first round trip on big sheets, and they never fell quiet (4 of 4). On a 48 sheet there is too little room for spirals, so only big sheets showed it. Inside, a cell still keeps 22 numbers per channel, 13 of them timers.

### Crossing

- **What the robot needs, and nature's ways.** Approach (Braitenberg's vehicles) needs each sensor to drive the thruster on the far side (thrusters push away from their side): crossed wiring, all twelve inputs' paths crossing at the centre. Nature: axons find targets by molecular tags (Sperry's chemoaffinity), mostly smooth gradients (graded Eph receptors against graded ephrins) rather than unique labels; unique identities (fly Dscam, protocadherins) serve self-avoidance; midline crossing is common (netrin attracts, then Slit/Robo prevents recrossing); later axons follow pioneers.
- **Crossing is free; routing to a target that isn't the nearest is what costs.** Tested by pulses on a robot-like ring (6 groups, a red and a blue input each, a thruster 3 cells in; each input's pulses should reach the opposite thruster only):
  1. **A channel per target:** 100%, no crosstalk, 3 seeds; paths in separate channels don't interfere even on shared cells, and diagonal paths cross at an X without sharing a cell. Cost grows with targets (6 channels).
  2. **Make the right target the nearest** (thrusters that pull: uncrossed wiring): 100% with one channel, 50 cells for all 12 inputs. A change of body, by far the simplest; ruled out only to stop evolution finding trivial wiring.
  3. **Rays (fly straight across, then follow breadcrumbs):** 100% without noise, but dead reckoning accumulates error: 3° wobble per step gave 63–68%, 6° about 50%. Re-aiming at the centre made it worse (the gradient flattens there).
  4. **A hub (chiasm):** paths meet at the centre, pulses go straight through, choosing at each branch the way that keeps their heading: 83–100% (mean 92%) with two channels however many paths; errors from paths wiggling into the hub.
  5. **Addresses (landmarks, like GPS):** four rim landmarks send waves; count differences give coordinates; a pulse heads for its target's address, then follows breadcrumbs. Five channels however many targets, closed-loop, general; 92% with exact distances, about 88% with noisy counts (after letting pulses cross plateaus and averaging maps over several waves).
  6. **One shared medium with labelled waves:** fails; waves from different sinks cancel where they meet, so a source never hears a target that isn't the nearest.
  7. **Untried, by reasoning:** layers with vias, or several breadcrumb slots per cell, solve crosstalk, which isn't the problem; "go to the farthest sink" has no simple local recurrence.
  Verdict, by simplicity then robustness: change the body (2), else a channel per target (1), else the hub (4) or addresses (5) at fixed cost and about 90%.

### Cuts, walls and one bit learned

- **Cuts and walls.** A cell that can't be on a path (a cut) ends the trail for good: what lies beyond vanishes and stays gone, a ready-made 1-bit memory. A cell waves can't pass (a wall) makes the path re-route round it within about 100 ticks.
- **One bit learned on a grown path (bare sheet).** A red and a blue input side by side, wired to one thruster; each input keeps a trace of its own recent pulses; visits in random order (one colour's pulses, then a taste at both inputs, bad after the poison colour); a bad taste while an input's trace is up closes its gate. In 4 of 4 seeds the poison colour's pulses stopped reaching the thruster after its first (once, second) visit while the other's kept arriving, and likewise with colours swapped. The gate must be at the input (paths from both inputs merge into a shared trunk, so a cut further on silences both), which is also where taste arrives in the robot. It is the three-factor rule (pulse × recent × taste) at the input; the path only routes.

### How we got here: earlier path rules

- **The flow rule (tag `exp/paths-flow`).** A chemical pulsed in at a source and drained at a sink, spreading through conductance g that grows with the flow through the cell faster than in proportion and decays slowly (canalisation, as auxin carves leaf veins and slime-mould tubes thicken with flow). Findings: g must be much slower than the chemical (else it collapses before the chemical crosses); the growth threshold sets the regime (no pruning; a thick "comet" whose width follows the load; at higher thresholds a lightning-like tree); a floor on conductance stops the sink being walled off before it can pull; weighting diagonal neighbours a quarter removed a 45° bias; with growth saturated nothing competed, and only a higher threshold gave a single thin line grown tip first. A harder drain didn't win the path (getting chemical through the floor cells round a sink was the bottleneck). Moved ends were rejoined by local repair, reusing the old path (hysteresis: economical, not shortest). Too slow: about 600,000 ticks per path (diffusion's square-root spread, plus wiring that must be slow and grows cell by cell), and speeding it up broke it.
- **The hops rule (distance-vector routing).** The minimum of the neighbours is as allowable as their mean: a cell holds its distance to the nearest sink (smallest neighbour's plus one step), settling in about one tick per cell, exact, self-repairing (Lee's algorithm; routers). A path follows the falling distance from the source: one cell wide in under 100 ticks. On the way: equal routes on a grid lit a whole parallelogram until roughness from birth broke the ties; jitter broke chains that needed every link's check to pass at once, and hysteresis fixed that but made braids and fans (no competition between neighbouring path cells). A moved sink left a dying valley ("count to infinity": a neighbourhood propping itself up, rising a step at a time); poisoning (a worsening distance jumps to unknown) cured it but, under lockstep, oscillated in a checkerboard (hence random updates); with no sinks at all, poisoned cells re-adopted stale values, and a refractory period after poisoning cleaned up but hurt joining. Each fix of count-to-infinity cost stability elsewhere: diminishing returns.
- **Growing a network joining every end.** Shortest-to-nearest was a side effect, not a goal: the robot must learn which sensor drives which thruster, so the aim became one network with junctions (close to a Steiner tree), learning at the junctions. Two maps (to sources and to sinks), paths merging at an anchored network. Fixes on the way: only growing tips may extend (else the network flooded 57–78% of the sheet); compare a neighbour's best step with one's previous distances (it is a tick behind). Following a neighbour's best next step was brittle under noise (tolerance too tight: nothing grows; too loose: blobs); choosing by name (exact tag match), only at the tip, and keeping only through-routes made networks thin (6 of 6 pairs without jitter, 3 of 6 with). The breadcrumb rule then replaced it all.
- **A wrong test, noted:** marking a cell as path when its distance to source plus distance to sink is no larger than its neighbours' smallest marks countless fragments (every cell is on its own best route). A path has to be grown from an end.

### Background reasoning on wiring

- **Where the information lives.** The hand-designed medium's function lived in the "enabled" map: wiring drawn from outside. A uniform sheet starting from noise has none, so all structure must be grown by the rule (development) and changed by experience (learning).
- **Fix the physics, evolve only knobs.** A general polynomial cell searches over all physics (excitable media, oscillators, endless rules that go quiet or flood). Choosing the form by hand (excitation, grown conductance, a taste-gated change) leaves 10–20 numbers and a rule that can be said in a sentence; a failure is then a clear result too.
- **Anti-diffusion makes a texture, not routing:** it grows the finest pattern the grid allows, everywhere. Spacing can come from an activator and an inhibitor (Turing; Gierer–Meinhardt), but a regular pattern still ignores what the ends need.
- **Purposeful paths come from flow (or demand).** Where something flows from sources to sinks, cells carrying more get better at carrying it, and broad flow narrows to paths (leaf veins, Physarum, Murray's law). The sink is what draws the wiring: wiring by demand, as targets release growth factors that the neurons wired to them depend on. Two routes to a sparse network, as in nature: start dense and prune, or grow up gradients and refine by activity; nervous systems do both, in that order.
- **One distance map per kind of end, not per end,** scales to many ends, and identical cells can't know which variable belongs to which end.

## Wiring drawn by hand (`old_manual_gates/`)

These count as capability experiments too; details in their own `LESSONS.md`.
- **Signal capabilities:** in a medium of one shared rule (an excitable cell; one dynamic variable is enough if it is a phase), with one parameter set derived from first principles, hand-drawn wiring gives propagation, one-way gates (narrow-to-wide geometry), AND, OR and XOR, crossings (from refractoriness), oscillators and inhibition. No clock; the price is races and timing windows.
- **Routing learned by reward:** "adaptation grows routes, then reward with a temperature selects them" learned and switched which of two inputs drives an output on 10 of 14 random sheets (`twophase.py`).
- **Not achieved:** AND by learning (every contributor must be credited, and each fix for one broke the other; random-order training killed routes for good), and learning in the robot (sparse reward, floods).
- **What carries over:** the wiring there was given; it is now what `paths.py` grows. The logic and crossings are capabilities grown wiring will need.

# Part 2: evolving the robot's cell rule

The earlier approach: one polynomial rule per cell variable, found by evolution through a curriculum (move, approach, graze, taste, choose, discriminate), in the robot body. Tagged `exp/robot-curriculum`.

## What evolution found and didn't

- **The body can give the answer away.** With each actuator pulling towards its own sensor's side, "copy your neighbour" was already a line-follower. Actuators are now thrusters (push away from their side), so approach needs signals to cross the disc.
- **Discrete time invites tricks.** With whole-step updates the evolved sheet flipped every cell each tick and steered by phase. A rate per variable (0.1) made firing a real excursion and recovery (6% of cells active while approaching).
- **Rules depend on the tick size unless evolved across it.** Approach and the first learner worked only at dt 1 (at 0.5 the sheet went quiet; at 2 approach ate 1.55 blocks instead of 2.6): their activity came from overshoot at the coarse step. Evolution exploits the finest step it is scored at: graze reached 13–16 meals at dt 1 and 0.5 but 2–5 at 0.25. Scoring at 1, 0.5 and 0.25 (raced) and validating at 0.125 keeps it honest.
- **Fixed evaluation worlds let flukes in.** The first "learner" (+0.48 on 16 worlds) scored 0 on fresh ones. Learning counts only when both halves of lives have meals, re-checked on fresh worlds.
- **Move and approach evolve in minutes with CMA-ES; learning didn't.** Taste and choose stayed on the "eat everything" plateau. Scoring the worse of a red-food and a blue-food life made standing still beat eating everything. The approach rule sits on a narrow ridge (at search spread 0.1 the search collapses to doing nothing). On a plateau CMA-ES's rankings are decided by noise.
- **A wide search found taste learning.** MAP-Elites (best rule per kind of behaviour; taste strengths as body genes) found within 1.5 hours a rule whose poison meals fell from 1.04 to 0.16 per half-life while food meals rose (128 fresh worlds); after a bad taste it kept away. Its memory: the taste cell that was kicked lowers its w and holds it; the slow variable m just settled to a constant offset in every cell (no differentiation into cell types). A taste in a non-learner leaves a chaotic, unusable difference.
- **Tick-robust learning needed taste on its own channel.** As a signed kick to v, 3 hours across tick sizes found nothing that survived fresh worlds; with a fourth variable t kicked only by taste (like a neuromodulator), MAP-Elites found one in 90 minutes: poison meals fall about 90% after the first bite at every tick size from 0.25 to 1 (food also falls 35–40%). Evolution put taste almost entirely at the point of contact.
- **It steers with travelling waves,** like the activation front across heart muscle: every cell takes part (18% active at any moment, a spike per cell every 18 time units). Charging spikes above one per 100 time units gave learners at about 2–2.6 (kept: `kernels/grid12/sparse_robust_learner.json`, +0.27 rise in food share at three tick sizes); still waves, thinner.
- **What the taste learners learn is a switch, not an association:** one bite lowers the robot's drive towards all blocks (sensitisation), so in forage it eats less of both colours.
- **We had been asking for one-shot learning by accident.** Lives of 1–3 meals gave a gradual learner almost no credit. A fair test needs tens of tastes of each colour, scored on how the life ends. Adding blocks didn't add meals (thrusters pushed against each other and the robot stalled): hence graze, then discriminate (long lives, score the last third and the improvement).
- **Choose and discriminate: nothing, repeatedly.** Choose: 287,000 candidates in 8 hours, no learner on fresh worlds. Discriminate on 12×12 stalled in 2 hours (88 niches); on 24×24, 10 hours, 33,600 candidates, 179 niches, best a dense forager of both colours; with taste at the inputs, 10 hours, 197 niches, best validated −0.02. Six variables (below), 9 hours: almost nothing searched.
- **Nothing asks for spiking or simplicity.** The rule is a general smooth equation; spiking is pushed only from outside (pulsed inputs; thrusters fire on crossing 0.5). The activity cost counts crossings, so a cell held high is free: it penalises oscillation, not activity. The coefficient penalty (0.001) is almost nothing. Levers if wanted: prune a working rule; thrusters proportional to v; charge activity itself.
- **The curriculum carries one lineage.** Each stage hands one rule on, so learning could only build on one forager chosen for eating, not for being teachable (busy and chaotic: its score swings with float rounding). `--seed` can carry several.

## The curriculum: growing the sheet, then adding variables

- **24×24 rather than smarter cells.** On 12×12, a sensor group's taste cell sat between its red and blue inputs and saw only their mean, and six groups were crammed onto 112 cells, leaving no room for structures. So the sheet grew to 24×24 (448 cells), sensor groups spread out. The 12×12 body also had a rounding fault (Python rounds halves to even: one group's thruster and taste cell were one cell); positions now sit just inside half-cell points and the body checks its special cells are distinct. 12×12 rules kept in `kernels/grid12/`.
- **Penalties tuned on the small sheet bit on the big one.** Per-cell activity costs (4× the cells) and the coefficient penalty outweighed anything approach could earn, so the search went quiet; and discriminate charged the whole life's activity against its last third's meals. Activity is now free in move and approach, charged over the scored third in discriminate.
- **The sheet builds circuits.** From near-blank noise an early approach rule organised 24×24 within a few dozen ticks into lasting structure like wires and junctions, similar but not identical across seeds: self-organisation, probably anchored by the body's special cells and the rim.
- **Stripes one cell wide** are the finest pattern the grid allows, favoured by neighbour-mean lateral inhibition: the spatial counterpart of the tick artefact, though alternating fates also arise in real tissue (Notch–Delta). If they get in the way, test on a hexagonal lattice.
- **24×24 graze reached 32 meals per 2,000 time units** (twice 12×12), but no colour learning.
- **48×48 was worse, and no sparser** (approach 1.24 against 2.7, graze 16 against 32, with a trimmed budget): signals have twice as far to cross, and no new structure appeared. Kept in `kernels/grid48/`. A much bigger sheet would need a GPU.
- **Taste now arrives at the input cells** (the bare-sheet experiments showed taste can't be carried far), the separate taste cell gone; graze unaffected.
- **Two variables (v, w) can move but hardly forage** (approach 0.46, graze 1.4): no base for learning. Kept in `kernels/vw_only/`.
- **Learning variables on top of the full forager.** Six variables (e fast, z slow, for e.g. an eligibility trace and a gate) and cubic terms in a cell's own variables: 882 coefficients; discriminate searched only the 622 terms involving e or z, the forager frozen. The first night searched almost nothing: the big loop body stopped vectorising (19× slower; fixed, see Speed), and random e and z terms at MAP-Elites' scales wrecked the forager (at spread 0.3, two thirds of its meals gone). For a non-learning forager the discriminate score is mostly noise while its meals are steady.
- **A target-rich world doesn't make random rules taste: most don't move.** 13 of 16 random rules fire their thrusters 7 times or fewer and then settle; whether a robot keeps moving is the first hurdle, and a cheap screen for hopeless candidates.

## Designing a seed rule by hand (bare-sheet experiments)

What uniform rule lets useful circuits form? Built step by step within the 4-variable quadratic cell on a bare 48×48 sheet (kept in `experiments/seed_design/`):
- **Excitable cells:** v grows above a threshold and decays below it (quadratic, with the clamp at 1 as the upper limit), and recovery w ends spikes. Recovery must be shunting (−B·v·w), or v goes below zero and rebounds; coupling must be excitatory input only, or a lone active cell drains before lighting its neighbours.
- **Canalisation by co-activity alone makes rings, not paths:** excitation moves as fronts, not flows, so there is no source-to-sink current to carve channels.
- **Channels by anti-diffusion work:** a labyrinth of one-cell channels from noise; channels conduct, insulators are inert; a pulse covers exactly its connected network.
- **A taste-gated cut is permanent but, in a labyrinth, not specific:** a connected labyrinth floods, so every cell has recently fired whichever input fired.
- **Taste can't be carried far:** one kick spread over a radius of 6 cells is about 0.01. It must arrive near the colour information.
- **Colour-specific learning from one bad taste:** with taste at the facing group's inputs and the labyrinth biased to break into short pieces, a bad taste after red cut red's channel and spared blue's in 9 of 11 groups, never both. Specificity needs local, non-flooding activity.
- **The tension:** fragmentation makes learning specific but channels short (about 13 cells at most), too short to cross the disc. In the robot the hand rule (`kernels/hand_seed.json`) doesn't move.
- **Why evolution didn't find it:** learning without eating scores like doing nothing; its ingredients are worthless alone (no stepping stones); its coefficients are large and specific (the cut is 300) where the search takes small, penalised steps; and it is a quiet excitable medium, while the curriculum digs into busy-forager territory.

## Cells, variables and meta-structures

- **Complexity per cell or in the arrangement of cells.** Rule 110 crosses signals in 1D because its information lives in gliders, meta-blocks of several cells; variables per cell are another way to add a dimension (a pulse in one layer passes under a pulse in another). Variables grow the rule quadratically and make search harder; a bigger sheet only costs compute. Prefer many simple cells; a working solution can later be translated into fewer, richer cells.
- **Meta-structures need a rule that supports them** (gliders, solitons, edge-of-chaos dynamics), room, and time. In a plain excitable medium colliding pulses annihilate.
- **Directional inputs aren't needed for particles:** Life is symmetric and totalistic, yet has gliders. A stripe, like a glider, has an orientation no single cell has.
- **The sparse learner has no travelling pulses to collide:** its sheet is never still, and a sensor kick locally disrupts the rhythm rather than launching a pulse; kicks on opposite sides just add.

## Speed

- **Cells as a list with neighbour lists, one term at a time across all cells, no allocation per tick:** 3.6× in one process, 2.4× on 8 cores.
- **8 cores give only 2.7× one core** (4 performance and 4 low-power cores, clock slowing under full load).
- **The cell step is 80–100% of a tick.** What made it faster, in order: a genome's lives stepped side by side (state variables × (cells × lives)); cell by cell rather than term by term over everything (data stays in cache); the rule written out per variable with coefficients copied to locals (the compiler keeps them in registers and vectorises 8 lives at a time: 5 ns per cell-life, near the floor). With six variables and cubics, one loop body for the whole rule stopped vectorising (263 ns); one loop per variable vectorises again (36 ns).
- **32-bit numbers,** with a random stream per life: together about 2× for a full evaluation, results unchanged.
- **Racing gave another ~3×:** a candidate's score is its worst over tick sizes, so live every candidate at dt 1 first and only the few that beat their niche's elite at finer steps. Racing on fewer worlds instead let noise reject the rare real improvement: the test must be exact, not cheap and noisy.
- **For the capability experiments:** Numba, with rows updated in parallel (each cell reads only the old state): 3.3× on `frame.py`; the live views budget their ticks per frame by time, so they never freeze.

## Related work

- **Neural Cellular Automata, the "lizard" (Mordvintsev et al., Distill 2020).** 16 variables per cell (RGBA plus 12 hidden); each cell sees its 8 neighbours only, through horizontal and vertical Sobel gradients (48 inputs), into one hidden layer (128 ReLUs, then back to 16): about 8,000 parameters, deep only by iteration. The last layer starts at zero (a new network does nothing at first). Each update is skipped with probability 0.5 (no clock); cells die unless a neighbour has alpha above 0.1. Trained by backpropagation through time. The hidden channels seem to encode where a cell belongs (one study: 3–6 effective dimensions; one channel like "edge", another perhaps the head-tail axis), and activity continues beneath the still image (suppressing it impairs repair).
- **The lizard's constraints against ours.**

  | | Lizard (Growing NCA) | Ours |
  |---|---|---|
  | Numbers a neighbour can read | all 16 (no private state) | 5 for the frame, 5 per path channel; private numbers allowed |
  | What a cell sees of its neighbours | each number through an identity, an east-minus-west and a south-minus-north filter (Sobel): 48 inputs | each neighbour's numbers, but no shared north (the rule treats every direction alike) and not which neighbours are edge cells |
  | A compass | yes: the Sobel filters are fixed to the grid, so every cell knows up from down and left from right | no: orientation must come from noise, and be broadcast |
  | Breaking symmetry | the compass plus a single seed cell in the middle: the lizard always grows the same way up | noise only: an election (now) or an instability (the 2-number frame being explored) |
  | Updates | each cell with chance 0.5 per step | the same idea: each cell with chance 0.5 per tick |
  | The rule | a trained network (48 → 128 ReLU → 16, about 8,000 weights), found by backpropagation through time | hand-designed, a sentence or two per stage |
  | Size | one size (the pattern is learned at a fixed scale) | any size, by rule |

  So "16 shared numbers, and each cell sees only its neighbours' averages" is half right: 16 shared, but it sees directional differences, which is a compass. The follow-up **Isotropic NCA** (Mordvintsev, Randazzo and Fouts, 2022) removes the compass (cells can no longer tell up from down) and must break symmetry exactly as we must: by structured seeds, or by training for any orientation and letting asynchronous updates choose one. **Steerable NCA** (2023) instead gives each cell an angle of its own to steer its perception.
- **For us:** a small ReLU network (about 210 parameters) would give thresholds directly, at the price of being harder to state and needing evolution (our simulation isn't differentiable). The Sobel gradients are the gradient inputs we set aside; 3–6 dimensions is close to our 4 variables.

## Ideas for future research

- **From dynamic to static memory.** A persisting oscillation costs energy, like short-term memory; if it gradually builds a trace in a slow variable that then stops the oscillator, the memory moves to a static substrate, invisibly to the rest of the network. Perhaps what representational drift is; it parallels working memory consolidated into synaptic change, and an activity cost would reward the shift.
- **Complexification.** Give each stage only the variables it needs, add one for the next challenge with the earlier coefficients frozen and the new ones starting at zero (NEAT, progressive networks, gene duplication).
- **Recruiting latent capability when failing.** A fast variable gated by a slow one that accumulates local evidence of failure (repeated bad tastes, surprise): metaplasticity; neuromodulators of uncertainty; silent synapses. In a quadratic cell a gate can scale one variable but not a product.
- **A learning variable and a variable that permits it.** Learning probably involves only a few cells, found locally by distance from the edge, closeness to sensors (a slow average of |t| marks exactly the cells that ever taste), or a marker from the body. Gating is three-way (zone × fired recently × taste), so it needs a cubic term or a trace variable.
- **Cubic terms** make excitability, three-factor learning and gating direct, but grow fast; a few chosen cubics are a middle way.
- **Finding the minimum number of variables** (once a learner exists): intrinsic dimensionality or a minimal realisation; measure by PCA or nonlinear estimators; reduce by rotating and truncating (a rotated quadratic rule stays quadratic), ablation, group sparsity or distillation. A variable may matter only in some cells and mean different things in different places (dopamine; superposition), which defeats a whole-sheet PCA: judge by regional ablation. Local rules can find principal components too (Oja, Sanger, Földiák).
