# Lessons

Alternatives we considered and why we settled where we did, so we don't re-litigate them.

The code for the earlier experiments (`learn.py` with the `learn1`/`learn2` patterns, `broadcast.py`, `attention.py`) was removed when learning moved into `physics.py`; it is in the git history.

## One dynamic variable per cell is possible, but only as a phase

- A single *level* per cell (like energy or flame alone) can't make a pulse: with no input it just drifts to rest, so it can't rise, fall, and then be refractory. That's why the current model needs two (flame fires, energy recovers).
- A single *phase* on a circle can do it: at rest until light reaches threshold, then it advances at a fixed rate back round to rest. Brightness is a fixed "light curve" of phase; only a resting cell can ignite.
- Everything demonstrated so far survives (it depends only on light geometry and refractoriness). The stuck-on trap, the fuel rule, re-entry and fizzles disappear, and there are about five parameters instead of eight.
- Lost: the fuel metaphor (recovery becomes a clock) and graded partial recovery.
- The light curve needs a finite rise, otherwise propagation speed depends on frame rate. Its rise sets hop time, its peak relative to the threshold sets the diode and AND margins, its bright length sets the coincidence window, and the dark rest of the cycle is the refractory period.
- Independent of this: `COUPLING_GAIN` is redundant, because it only ever acts through the ratio `MIN_STRIKE / COUPLING_GAIN`.
- A per-cell phase is not a clock. It is a one-shot timer started by that cell's own ignition; there is nothing global or synchronised.

## No global clock

- Core thesis: local behaviour only, no global god. A global clock is exactly that god, so it is ruled out.
- With a clock, everything we've built would carry over (Wireworld, a synchronous cellular automaton, has diodes, logic, crossings and a computer). Our gates depend only on threshold excitation, refractoriness and local coupling, not on continuous time.
- A clock would remove our races and hazards (veto-lead windows, coincidence windows, crossing dead time and misroutes), because "at the same time" becomes "on the same tick". We accept those as the price of asynchronous, local timing.
- Continuous asynchronous time also keeps graded timing differences available to learning rules.

## Learning: where the change lives, and what triggers it

- A cell can tell three things locally: *was I involved* (light arriving, recent firing, low energy), *was it good* (nothing yet: needs a teaching signal), and *how much to change* (a new slow per-cell value).
- Teaching signal as a second kind of light (a "modulator"), rather than a special input on the ordinary light: it never excites, it only switches plasticity on. So a teacher can't itself leak through as a pulse. A modulator is just another cell's signal on a second channel.
- Eligibility by light or by energy are mirror images. Receiver learns to listen: light reaching me now, a near-miss, lowers my threshold. Sender learns to shout: my energy is low (I fired recently), raises my brightness. Energy can't mark a blocked junction (it never fires), but tolerates a teacher arriving seconds late.
- "Was I used?" is available for free: light from others *after* I ignite. Measured on a line: used cells get about twice the neighbour light over their burn of a dead-end cell, from about one hop after ignition. It says "used", not "useful", and correlation can fake it.
- Illumination counts other cells only. A cell's own light never affected ignition (only dark cells ignite), but it swamped any rule or measurement reading a burning cell's light. Light from others before I ignite is the *cause*; after, the *echo*.

## Learning experiments (T1 single junction, T2 dual input; learn.py)

- **Reciprocity.** With light and teaching signal sharing a reach, a transducer close enough to teach junction J is lit by J just as strongly. A diagonal transducer got ignited by J's own firing, so plain use of A re-taught A and switching to B never happened. Put the transducer two cells from J (J's light there ≈ 0.18 × threshold) and raise the learning rate to compensate.
- **Only "ready" cells should learn:** dark, with at least `STRIKE_LEVEL` energy. A weight only matters at ignition. Letting every cell learn strengthened the teacher's own line and shifted its timing. Dark-only still punished the transducer's feeder in the moment it was exhausted but the transducer was still burning, cutting off the teacher's supply.
- **Constants in light units are gain-sensitive.** A fixed offset or darkness cut-off shifts balance when all light scales by ±20%. Scale-free alternative: compare light with the teaching signal itself, `dw = M(η·I − κ·M)`. Taught junction I/M ≈ 4.4, untaught ≈ 0.55, so κ/η = 1.5.
- **With ready-only learning, a junction stops learning the instant it fires,** so its first success has zero margin. Margin grows with further pairings (each fires a little earlier on the rising light), so train for a fixed number of pairings, not until first success.
- **Learning speed scales with gain²** (light and teaching signal each scale with gain): −20% gain learns about 2.5× slower.
- Letting burning cells keep strengthening (to build margin) strengthened the teacher's own line too, so L's pulse arrived earlier and fell out of step with A.

## What is designed and what is learned (as of learn1/learn2)

- Designed by hand: junction geometry (input ends diagonally beside the junction, ≈ 0.7 × threshold), transducer placement (two cells from the junction), matched path lengths so input and teacher coincide, and a teacher that fires at exactly the right moment.
- Learned: only whether each designed junction conducts.
- So the designer still does most of the work. Next steps remove pieces of it: a teacher that arrives *after* the action (needs an eligibility trace), then junctions nobody designed, then teaching with no teacher at all.

## Reward timing: traces, not order

- Anticipating a reward and receiving it are the same signal (dopamine reward-prediction error: after learning, the burst moves to the earliest reliable predictor). So the rule shouldn't care whether the teacher comes before or after the activity, only whether they're close in time.
- Each cell keeps a fading trace of the brightest recent light and teaching signal it received (peak-hold, fading with `TRACE_TIME`); learning uses the traces. A near miss now leaves a wake, which energy alone can't (a cell that doesn't fire never uses energy).
- Traces broke the teacher again: the transducer's feeder recovers enough to ignite (`STRIKE_LEVEL`) about 0.8 s after burning out, while the teaching trace still lingers. Requiring full energy ("at rest") fixed that, but then a junction that fired on the input was refractory when a late teacher arrived, and couldn't be credited. Splitting eligibility fixed both: every cell strengthens for light it received; only cells at rest weaken.
- `TRACE_TIME` sets how close counts as "together". 2 s gives a robust window from 0.5 s early to 1.5 s late. 4 s widened it, but the trace was still about 17% strong when the feeder fully recovered, breaking learn1.
- Noise will matter (exploration: letting near-miss paths occasionally fire), but not yet: it makes training non-deterministic. Noise on received light would be targeted (only near misses fire), and would need its own timescale to stay frame-rate independent.
- Possible later: make ignition itself integrate light (like a membrane potential), so the trace and the firing mechanism are one; or hold a spread of activation levels per cell. Both change every gate's dynamics, so parked.

## Broadcast reward in a random medium (overnight experiments; broadcast.py)

- **A flooding medium can't assign credit from a broadcast reward.** In a filled-in random patch, a pulse sweeps everything as one wavefront; when an output fires, the cells active in the last few seconds are a band right across the medium, including the routes to the other output. Timing is the only thing a broadcast carries, and in a flood it doesn't separate routes. Punishment then tipped the medium into total silence, which is absorbing: no activity, no reward, no recovery.
- **Excitable propagation in a connected medium is all-or-none** (a wave sweeps the whole connected cluster or dies), so per-cell homeostasis on a filled-in medium just cycles it between flood and silence. Sparse activity needs weak links everywhere: a network of thin wires touching only diagonally, where every contact is a potential synapse that conducts only if the receiving cell's weight is ≈ 1.4 or more.
- **Input and output wires shouldn't be homeostatic;** the input fires every trial by definition, so homeostasis just suppresses it. But the outputs' synapses must be plastic, or the network can never learn to reach them.
- **Homeostasis toward a target firing rate starves unlit regions:** silent cells far from any activity saturate at the weight cap, then flood when activity finally reaches them. Adapting only cells that received light (no evidence, no change) grows activity outward instead, but with each junction settling at the target transmission rate, the chance of reaching depth d falls like target^d: exploration can't reach distant outputs.
- **Full sheet, weights as the only gate:** every cell enabled, weights allowed down to 0 (off). This unifies "enabled" with weight. It floods at first and, with a wide random spread of starting weights (±0.5), no longer dies completely, but reward-plus-homeostasis only occasionally reached "x only" (1 of 6 random sheets; 0 of 6 without reward). Homeostasis re-sensitised the barrier cells that pruning had created, reopening the pruned route.
- **Trial-and-error needs the trials to differ.** A broadcast reward is one number for the whole network; it can only credit routes if some trials reach one output and not the other. In deterministic dynamics with fixed weights every trial is the same, and a flood reaches both outputs at once, so reward and punishment land on the same cells and cancel. Variability between trials (reproducible, from a seeded source) is the missing ingredient, not a detail.
- **What worked (first emergent learning):** a full sheet held near the edge of firing, varied trials, and a surprise signal. Starting weights about 0.4 ± 0.15; the input wire extended 6 cells into the sheet so the pulse enters as a front, not through one cell; a fresh random offset (±0.1, seeded) on every weight each trial; a broadcast of sign × (1 − p), where p is how often that output has been firing; each cell's weight changes by that times how recently it fired (2 s trace). Result: "x only" learned on 19/20 random sheets and "y only" (reward reversed) on 17/20, against 0/20 without reward, in 150 trials.
- **Near the edge, the bottleneck was the entry.** A cell fed by a single wire end needs weight ≈ 0.7; inside the sheet a front gives ≈ 3 neighbours' worth of light, so it keeps going down to ≈ 0.35. With a single-cell entry, trials were all-or-none ("nothing" or "both"). Feeding the sheet along a line made interior outcomes vary ("x only", "y only", both, none), which is what reward needs.
- **Adding more mechanisms made it worse:** conservation (4/6 → 2/6 when combined) and global homeostasis (4/6) both reduced success at this stage. Prediction error was essential: plain reward gave 0/6, because x fired on most trials and every active cell was rewarded.
- **Two inputs (a → x, b → y) failed: 0/10 random sheets, and 0/32 with longer traces (4 s, 8 s) or a slower rate.** Nearly every trained sheet gives the *same* answer for a and b. With one scalar weight per cell, a sheet can only become a fixed set of open and closed cells, and near the edge of firing both inputs flood the same open region. "x only" (R1) could be learned by walling off y close to y, where the credit lands. The straight mapping needs a wall across the middle, and nothing can learn it:
  - by the time an output fires, the middle cells fired seconds earlier, so the credit misses them (a flood takes 7–16 s to cross; the fronts reach x and y within 0.4–2.5 s of each other);
  - every cell fires in both a-trials and b-trials, so which cells fired says nothing about which input was used;
  - one cell switched off by exploration can't block a flood; only a whole wall can, and that almost never happens by chance.
  The crossed mapping (a → y, b → x) is impossible for any fixed wall pattern (planarity).
- **There's no sparse middle ground.** With weights at 0.36 or below, activity dies within about 5% of the sheet; at 0.4 it floods about 90%. That is percolation, so tuning the starting weight can't give sparse, input-specific activity.

## Paying attention: credit runs back along the route (attention.py)

- **Idea:** the teacher pays attention to the output it wants when that output fires. A cell that is paid attention passes it on to its *causes*: the neighbours that fired first before it. Cells that are paid attention strengthen; cells that fire without being paid attention weaken slightly (firing costs, being useful pays). There is no global signal.
- **This is the timing version of "follow the falling forward gradient and the rising backward gradient".** The forward gradient is when each cell fired (the wave's arrival time); the backward gradient is the attention relay. Along a shortest route the two sum to a constant, so each step back from the output, the forward time drops by one hop. Passing attention to the earliest-firing neighbour follows exactly that (steepest descent of arrival time, as in excitable-media maze solving). Because it selects by timing, **a flood no longer hides the route:** attention picks out a thread of about 30–40 cells from about 1,000 that fired.
- **Result:** from a flooding start (weights 0.45 ± 0.15), with cost 0.002 per unattended firing, +0.1 per attention, and causes being any earlier neighbour within 30% of the earliest's lead: a → x learned on 16/16 random sheets, and the straight mapping (a → x, b → y) on 16/16, against 0/16 for both controls (the same with no attention), in 200 trials. It is deterministic, with no exploration noise. Broadcast reward got 0/10 on the straight mapping.
- **Crossed (a → y, b → x) fails, 0/16, as the planarity argument predicts:** it learns two wires that cross, and with one weight per cell a crossing leaks both ways, so both inputs reach both outputs. It needs per-link (directional) weights, which attention supplies naturally ("the neighbour that lit me" is a link).
- **Things that mattered:**
  - A 1-cell thread must be able to carry the pulse on its own once the flood recedes. A step lit diagonally by one neighbour needs weight ≈ 1.4 (diagonal coupling is about half the orthogonal), so the output's first cell, which was fixed at 1.0, blocked it. Making it plastic (it is the output's synapse onto the sheet) fixed it.
  - Attention can only start if the output fires at least once. At weights 0.40 the first flood missed x on 3/8 sheets; 0.45 always reaches it. Exploration noise (±0.05–0.1) also rescues most sheets, but ±0.2 made every trial reach both outputs.
  - A cost that is too high (0.005) sometimes shrinks the flood before the thread can conduct alone.
- **Decision: no directional (per-link) weights.** A flat 2-D medium can't cross routes with one weight per cell, and we accept that. Crossing, where needed, comes from clusters of cells and inhibition (as in `cross`). Keeping each cell to one weight matters more.

## Two loops: adaptation grows routes, reward with a temperature selects them (twophase.py)

- **Test:** a random sheet with inputs a and b and one output o. Phase 1 (adaptation) grows routes from both inputs to o. Phase 2 is learn2's test, done by reward: teach a (a passes, b doesn't), then teach b (b passes, a doesn't). No change without the teacher.
- **The teacher only rewards what the network just did; it never makes the output fire** (that would be hand-holding). So the network must sometimes act on its own: exploration needs randomness.
- **A random patch has no near misses** (12×12, 20 sheets per weight): a pulse either dies near the entry or sweeps across. The teacher can only work on routes that already reach o, which is why adaptation has to come first.
- **Routes conduct both ways.** Once a's and b's routes join, a's pulse flows back down b's route, so b's junction is never "at rest" and the old exclusivity rule (weaken cells at rest near the teacher) can't block it. Capping weights so a cell needs two lit neighbours (a diode everywhere) gave either one route only, or wide merged routes; never two separate ones.
- **What worked for blocking: "did I cause the output?", not "did I fire?".** On reward, attention traces back from o through the causes of its firing; causes strengthen, and cells *beside* the route that fired without causing it (backflow, side leaks) weaken. That gates b's branch exactly at the join, wherever it is, with no diode. Crediting near misses (lit, didn't fire) undid it: a closed gate is itself a near miss beside the rewarded route. A cell that didn't fire did nothing.
- **Temperature:** a cell at rest ignites by chance at 5/s × exp((x − 1)/T), with x = weight × light / threshold; T = 0 is the deterministic physics. Teaching hot and using cold (T = 0.1, then 0): teaching a works on 14/14 sheets where adaptation succeeded, and the full switch a → b on 6/14 (16 sheets, 150 trials each). T = 0.15 made every gate leak; T = 0.08 rarely switched. A teacher that rewards only a response due from the input (1–6 s after it) was needed: otherwise it rewards chance firings of o.
- **How switching happens (traced):** teaching a closes b's route at one or two cells at the join (e.g. weight 0.32 → 0.17); the rest of b's route stays at full weight. b gets through only when a gate cell fires by chance while b's pulse is beside it; each success is rewarded, and on sheets that switch, successes snowball (80–105 of 150 b-trials rewarded). On sheets that don't, b gets through only 1–10 times in 150 trials and never snowballs, while rewards traced to chance sparks and to activity left over from the previous trial (fixed 10 s trials) strengthen a's route and punish b's gate again.
- **Attention must reach as far as light.** A spark lit across a one-cell gap has its cause two cells away; attention that only looked at immediate neighbours stopped at the spark and credited a's route instead of b's. Looking two cells out when no neighbour fired earlier fixed the tracing, but not the success rate (5/16): the bottleneck is how rarely b gets through.
- **Trials must start from a reset, not by waiting for quiet** (a sheet that loops, or is kept busy by chance firing, never goes quiet). With a reset (flames out, energy full; weights kept): b passes reliably after adaptation (the earlier 0.8 was leftover activity), teaching a works on 14/14, and the full switch on 6/14 at T = 0.1 and at T = 0.12; T = 0.15 leaks everywhere. At T = 0.12 the sheets that don't switch still collect 70–100 rewards in 150 b-trials, so rewards are not scarce: they are going to the wrong route.
- **A local thermostat helps, if it is capped below the leak point.** Each cell has its own teaching temperature: it warms (+0.01 per trial) when it was lit or fired but attention neither reached it nor passed next to it, and drops back to the base when attention reaches it. Base 0.05, cap 0.12: the full switch on 10/14 sheets (6/14 at the best fixed temperature); teaching a still 14/14. Two wrong versions first: heating every lit, unrewarded cell heated the closing gate itself (it is lit by a's backflow), so it fired early, became a "cause" and never closed (0/16); and a cap of 0.15–0.2, above the fixed temperature at which every gate leaks, ran away (hot cells fire, light their neighbours, which heat too) (0/16).
- **What the thermostat's gain comes from** (16 sheets): heat that never fades 10/16; heat that fades back to the base (10% per trial) 8/16; no heating, just "every cell warm (0.12), cells attention reaches drop to 0.05 and drift back" 7/16; fixed temperature 6/16. So keeping the rewarded route cool explains a little, and heating cells whose activity goes unrewarded adds most of the rest; letting heat fade lost some. With 14 usable sheets, differences of two or three are within chance. Watching it live: the heat is not concentrated at the gate as intended; it spreads over the competing input's route (lit by backflow, never rewarded) and never falls.
- **The switch between teaching and use is a god.** The experiment runs hot on teaching trials and at T = 0 in use; the cells can't know which. Either the teacher's presence must be an input that cells receive (a "teacher is watching" signal), or temperature must always follow local reward history, and behaviour in use then drifts towards exploration.

## How a cell can tell whether it is doing a good job

It can't judge itself in isolation; "good" is relative to what is valued downstream. What it can observe locally is whether it fired and whether attention came back to it, passed close by, or came nowhere near:

| | attention came back to me | attention passed close by, not to me | no attention near |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I fired** | useful: strengthen, cool | interfering (backflow, side leak): weaken | frustrated: warm up, explore |
| **I stayed dark** | — | correctly quiet: nothing | no information |

Doing a good job = my activity is followed by attention coming back to me: attention earned causally (from a cell that fired because of me) and flowing only from valued outcomes. Weight is what the cell does about it; temperature is its running self-assessment. "No information" is where the teach/use question lives: in use every cell is there.
- **Heat only near misses, and let it fade.** Watching it live showed the broad rule was wrong: heat spread 3–6 cells around every active path and stayed there. Cells that warmed because they fired by chance lit their neighbours, which warmed too, so heat fed on its own noise; cells that far out can't conduct, so their chance firing is pure noise. Warming only cells that were lit but didn't fire, with heat fading 5% per trial: from 15% of threshold, 13/16 (13 of the 14 sheets where routes grew); from 25%, 11/16; from 50%, 4–6/16, because after teaching a the gates get only 10–70% of their threshold light from the stopped pulse; without fading, 9/16.
- **"Attention passed close by" must mean within light's reach (two cells), like attention's own reach.** With only immediate neighbours, a cell lit by a working route from two cells away was a near miss on every trial and never excused, and sat pinned at the maximum temperature (live, one isolated cell at a time). With two cells: still 13/16, and no stuck cells.

## Attention passed cell to cell (twophase.py, local=True)

- **Each cell has a fire trace** (1 when it ignites, fading with a 3 s time constant): how long ago it fired, readable by its neighbours. No clock: two traces fade at the same rate, so their ratio says only how far apart the two cells fired.
- **One hop per time step:** an attended cell finds its neighbour that fired first before it (two cells out if none adjacent, as light reaches) and emits an acceptance level (trace^tol × earliest^(1 − tol)); a neighbour whose own trace is at or below it becomes attended. A cell that fired next to an attended cell and is never accepted is a leak.
- **A cell must listen only to the attended neighbour that fired soonest after it** (the one it could have caused). Taking the most permissive level from any attended neighbour let backflow cells at the join in on the say-so of cells further down the route that fired after them: they were credited instead of blamed, and a no longer blocked b (11/16).
- **Result: 12/16, against 13/16 for attention traced back by the experiment code** (one sheet differs: teaching a didn't block b). The local version often accepts a few more cells beside the route (up to 31 against 17).
- Still not local in time: weight changes and blame are applied at the end of the trial, and "never accepted" is judged then.

## Purely local: the learning rule in physics.py

Everything the cells do is now in `physics.py`; outside the cells there are only strikes (the inputs, and a teacher cell beside the output, struck when the output responds), and a `teaching` input that switches learning on. No resets: the grid runs continuously, one input pulse every 12 s.

- **The teacher is a cell.** It gives no light and is never lit, so it can't be set off by the output (that would reward everything). When struck it is attended, and it accepts *every* neighbour that fired before it, not just the earliest: nothing lit it, and its "causes" are what it saw. With the ordinary rule it passed over the output itself, and the output was then blamed as a leak on every reward until it stopped firing.
- **Attention needs refractoriness, like a flame.** With attention simply lasting a second, cells could be accepted again after it lapsed, and chance firing kept re-igniting attended cells, so attention circulated through the flood for the whole trial, re-crediting hundreds of cells (average weight 0.5 → 1.9 in 20 trials). After passing attention on, a cell is now refractory for 8 s: one teacher strike, one wave.
- **Base temperature near zero (0.01).** At 0.05 (fine when adaptation ran without chance firing) chance firing kept the flood alive and got credited: after adaptation both inputs lit 55–75% of the sheet, the same cells, so there was no join to gate. At 0.01 only heated near misses fire by chance, and the flood recedes to 12–19%.
- **A leak is any cell that fired beside the credited route without being accepted, including the flanks.** Restricting blame to cells that fired at least 0.1 s after their credited neighbour (backflow only, to spare flanks during adaptation) stopped teaching a from blocking b on almost every sheet: blocking relies on blaming the cells alongside the route at the join. With the flood kept small by the low base temperature, flank blame no longer kills adaptation.
- **The teacher's response window must allow for slow early floods:** at the starting weights o fires 6.0–6.7 s after the input on some sheets; with a 1–6 s window they were never rewarded and died. 1–9 s.
- **Faster heating (0.02 per second of unrewarded near miss, from 0.01) rescued most of the sheets where b never got through.** Stronger blame (0.1) instead broke adaptation. Result, all local: routes grow on 16/16 sheets, teaching a blocks b on 15/16, the full test passes on 14/16 (the version with attention traced back by experiment code: 13/16).

## No hidden scale: causes are judged between neighbours

- **An absolute "fired recently" cutoff (trace above 5%, i.e. within about 9 s) is a hidden scale:** it caps how long a route can take, so a bigger sheet would need a longer trace. Cells must not know how big their world is. Instead a cause is a neighbour that fired within CAUSE_WINDOW (1 s, about a flame's lifetime) *before* the cell, and a leak one that fired within CAUSE_WINDOW of an attended neighbour. Both are ratios of two traces, so they depend only on how far apart two cells fired, never on how long ago: a route can be any length. Result at 32×32: verify 8/8, 16 sheets 13/16 (14/16 before).
- **Only the experimenter's timing scales with the sheet:** a sheet twice as wide takes twice as long to cross, so the gap between input pulses and the teacher's response window double. The cells know nothing of it.
- **At 64×64 (150 teaching trials): 2/8.** Routes grow on 7/8 and teaching a blocks b on 5/8, but switching back is weak. Whether blocking works on a given sheet depends on its history: the same sheet blocked b in one run and not in another.

## AND: why the rules that learn routing can't learn AND (not solved)

Test: adapt as before (a and b each rewarded), then rotate a+b together (rewarded if o responds), a alone and b alone (punished if o responds: a BAD teacher cell beside o, whose attention wave takes weight away instead of adding it).

- **Plain reward and punishment:** within 30 trials one input is blocked, but the other still reaches o alone after 300 trials. On a+b trials attention credits only the input whose pulse reached o first (a cause is the neighbour that fired *first*), so that input's route gains on a+b trials faster than punishment takes it away, and the other input is only ever punished.
- **The physically right notion of cause for a junction that needs both inputs is every neighbour still burning when it ignited** (the later arrival is the one that tips it over): "tolerance 1" in the attention rule. But in a flood that credits every cell of the front, about 240 cells per reward instead of 40: the sheet over-strengthens and stays flooded, and adaptation fails.
- **Stronger punishment (0.3)** destroys both routes before the rare a+b rewards can build anything.
- **Scaling blame by how marginal a cell's firing was** (a cell driven far past its threshold would have fired anyway). Drive measured at ignition is always just above 1 (light rises gradually, so a cell ignites the moment it crosses); the peak drive while burning does vary (0.9 to 4). Scaling credit by it killed adaptation (flood cells all look redundant); scaling only punishment changed nothing.
- **Attention as a fixed amount shared among causes** (so it can credit every contributor without spreading through floods): shares shrink so fast near the output that no route ever gets enough credit, and adaptation fails.
- **The conflict:** growing a route out of a flood needs credit concentrated on one thin thread, which works only because attention ignores most contributors; AND needs every contributor credited. Each variant that helps one breaks the other. Removed again (drive, margin, sharing); kept the BAD teacher and valence.
- **Growing routes from a quiet sheet instead of pruning a flood** (start just below the level where pulses sweep the sheet, so activity stays sparse and "every contributor is a cause" might be safe). From 0.33 chance firing never reaches the output. From 0.36 with every contributor credited, credit spreads through the chance-fired activity much as through a flood: the average weight climbs to 0.8–1.25 and the output ends up either silent or firing by itself. From 0.36 with the usual rule, one sheet grew a route for one input only.
- **64×64 with 300 teaching trials per input: 3/8** (150 trials: 2/8). Teaching a now blocks b on 7/8 (150: 5/8), so blocking just needed longer at that size. Still failing: switching back to b opens b without closing a (2 sheets), b never gets through (1), adaptation never grows b's route (1).

## Local winner-take-all

- **Surround inhibition (a "Mexican hat": excitation near, weaker inhibition further out) does not make activity sparse in this medium.** A pulse still either dies or floods the sheet (2–12% or 73–99% lit); inhibition only moves the threshold between the two, because a plane front has the same density of burning cells all along it, so no part of it wins. It also broke `cross`, `inhibit` and `xor` (parallel lines inhibit each other). A symmetric kernel can't tell "ahead of me" from "beside me". Removed.
- **Where winner-take-all does belong is credit.** Deciding a cell's causes is a local winner-take-all among its inputs: the earliest-firing neighbour wins, and the tolerance says how close a rival must be to share. A softer one (tolerance 0.5–0.6) didn't help AND and hurt switching.
- **Blame goes to the weakest link: a winner-take-all over blame along the route.** On a designed AND junction (the `and` gate, junction weight raised so single inputs pass), plain punishment hits every cell on the route, and the output line, which fires on every trial, dies. What marks the junction is that on single-input trials it fires *late* after its cause (its light only just reaches threshold), and on a+b trials fast. That delay is already in the traces: the ratio of a cell's trace to its earliest cause's. Attention now carries the slowest delay met so far on its way back from the teacher, and a cell takes the credit or punishment only if it is slower than that (by 2%). Peak drive while burning was useless for this: once a cell fires, its downstream neighbours light it back up, so the peak says nothing about how close its own decision was.
- **Result, designed junction starting at 1.6 (without firing cost or leak blame): AND learned.** The junction weight settles at 1.0, inside its window (single ≈ 0.7 of threshold at weight 1, so the window is about 0.7–1.4), and stays: a+b 10/10, a 0/10, b 0/10, for steps of 0.05 and 0.1. Starting at 2.0 it never moves: at that weight the junction fires from one input as fast as a line cell, so it is never the weakest and the blame lands elsewhere. The firing cost wore every line cell down over 450 trials.
- **Weakest-link credit on random sheets:** with firing cost or leak blame, adaptation dies (routes never get strong before the flood is worn away); without them, the flood never recedes, no junction becomes marginal, and neither blocking nor AND is learned.
- **Reliability decides the breadth of credit:** a cell whose weight is below RELIABLE takes credit whenever attention reaches it (broad, as adaptation needs); above it, only as the weakest link. The weight itself is the reliability: no new state. With RELIABLE 1.3, adaptation worked on 4/4 sheets and switching 3/4, but AND still failed. With it, crediting every contributor (tolerance 1) no longer breaks adaptation (the weakest-link rule limits who takes credit), but AND still fails: one input keeps passing alone, or everything dies.
- **Why AND fails on random sheets (hypothesis):** after adaptation the two inputs' routes merge into one trunk before the output. On a+b trials the first pulse to reach the merge goes on to the output and the second arrives at cells that are still refractory, so no cell ever needs both. A junction that sums two inputs needs them to arrive from different sides within about a flame's lifetime, and nothing in adaptation selects for that timing.

## Speed: the per-cell rules compiled

- Profiling showed about 2.7 ms per step on a 32×32 sheet, three quarters of it in attention, and almost all of that NumPy's fixed cost per call: about 116 whole-array neighbour operations per step, each on only 1,024 cells. A GPU would be slower still at this size (bigger fixed cost per operation); batching the neighbour operations in NumPy gave only 1.2×.
- `physics.py` is now plain loops over cells, compiled by Numba: bit-identical results (every array, over whole runs, with chance firing), 20× faster at 32×32, 7–8× at 64 and 128. Skipping the attention scan when nothing is attended (most steps) is part of that. `verify.py` takes about 70 s instead of about 8 minutes.

## AND on random sheets: where it stands

- **After adaptation, a lights exactly the cells b lights** (about 50–60): each pulse reaches o and then runs backwards down the other input's route to its input. The two routes are one path a ↔ o ↔ b, merging into a shared trunk before o, so no cell ever needs both inputs.
- **Inputs on opposite sides** (a from the left, b from the right, o at the bottom middle) make the routes meet above o from opposite sides, the right geometry for a junction. It changed nothing on its own: one input's route is credited on every a+b trial (its pulse reaches o first) and survives its punishment; the other's is only punished and is cut.
- **Blocking single inputs first** (punish them before any a+b trial) kills everything: the cut lands on the shared trunk and o's wire, which every single-input trial uses. With the default rule every route cell lost about 1.0 of weight; with weakest-link blame restricted by RELIABLE, blame spread over the whole region. A crafted order is anyway not acceptable: training must work with inputs in random order.
- **Teaching AND from a fresh sheet** (no adaptation stage; from the first trial a+b rewarded, singles punished), with every contributor credited (tolerance 1) and inputs on opposite sides: **AND learned on 6/32 sheets** with a fixed rotation (a+b, a, b, ...). The rest die (21) or leave one input passing alone (5).
- **With the inputs in random order: 0/32**, and 0/96 across starting weights 0.45–0.55 and punishment 0.05–0.1. A fresh sheet is barely above the level where pulses die out, so a run of punished single-input trials drops a route below it; once a+b can't reach o nothing is ever rewarded again, so the death is permanent. The fixed rotation survives only because it interleaves exactly one reward between every two punishments. Repeating an input with learning off is fine.
- **So the obstacle is not credit assignment but recovery:** punishment can destroy structure that reward then has no way to rebuild. Exploration (chance firing at heated near misses) is not enough to bring a dead route back.
